ED Triage Disposition Checklist for Incidental Cerebrovascular Findings

🔒  No patient identifiers.  Enter clinical details only — everything stays on this device.
1
Any red-flag symptoms right now?
Checked first. If any is present the patient goes back to the ED before the aneurysm details matter. Leave all unchecked if none.
Thunderclap headache with no prior LP = bleeding not excluded → return to ED. A completed, negative LP lets the tool continue to the lesion.
2
Where is the finding?
Cavernous-segment lesions sit outside the dura (essentially no bleed risk); posterior-circulation lesions are treated more urgently.
3
What does it look like on imaging?
The read, the size, and the shape together set the baseline urgency.
mm
10 mm or larger is an automatic return to the ED. Quick buttons enter a representative value — type the exact size if you have it.
Under 3 mm · smooth funnel · branch vessel from the apex · typical branch point · no bleb or lobulation. Meeting every one is what separates a benign infundibulum from a small aneurysm.
3
Imaging details do not change the plan here
For a cavernous-segment lesion, size and shape do not affect the disposition — this segment lies outside the dura, so there is no risk of a subarachnoid bleed. The plan is set entirely by the cavernous-sinus symptoms question in Step 2.
4
Vascular risk factors
Two or more marks the patient higher-risk and can raise urgency one step. Check all that apply; leave empty if none.
5
Patient age
Fine-tunes the recommendation — younger keeps a lower threshold; the very elderly with a small benign-looking lesion may need only reassurance. Never overrides a return-to-ED.
years
Recommendation
🩺
Answer the steps on the left, then tap Get recommendation.
The disposition, what to do, and the reasoning will appear here.
Decision support only — not a substitute for clinical judgment. Assumes an incidental finding with no acute bleeding in a discharged, stable patient. Any acute or deteriorating presentation is an emergency regardless of this output. The protocol uses no absolute rules; cases outside it are flagged for expert review rather than guessed.
